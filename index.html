1. More rows, more counters?

Mia makes 4 rows of 6 counters. Leo makes 3 rows of 8 counters.

Mia says, “I have more counters because I have more rows.”

Is she right? Explain how you checked.

2. Finish the tiled floor

Each square below is the same size.

  • ■ means a tile is already in place.
  • □ means an empty space that needs a tile.

How many tiles must you add to complete the rectangle?

What will the total area be, measured in square tiles, when it is finished?

3. Sharing a collection

Use 18 counters, or draw 18 berries.

  • Share them equally among 3 children. Each child receives one third of the collection.
  • Now start again with all 18 and share them equally among 2 children. Each child receives one half.

Which is more: one third of 18 or one half of 18? How many more counters is it?

4. Make it certain—or not!

You may draw stars or circles on cards. Each card has one picture, and all cards have identical backs.

You will mix the cards and pick one without looking.

Make three separate sets of 3 cards:

  • In Set A, picking a star must be certain: it must happen.
  • In Set B, picking a star must be possible but not certain: it could happen, but might not.
  • In Set C, picking a star must be impossible: it cannot happen.

Draw or describe each set. You may repeat pictures.

5. Arrive, then wait

A train leaves at 9:45 am. The journey takes 35 minutes.

The museum beside the arrival station opens at 10:30 am. There is no walking time to add.

What time does the train arrive? How many minutes must you then wait for the museum to open?

6. Same beads, different necklaces

You have 32 beads.

Compare these two plans:

  • Use 4 beads per necklace.
  • Use 8 beads per necklace.

Use all the beads each time.

Which plan makes more necklaces? How many more necklaces does it make?

7. A piece can contain smaller pieces

The entire piece shown below is one third of a whole chocolate bar. It contains two small squares.

All the small squares in the whole bar are the same size.

Draw or describe the whole bar. How many small squares does it contain altogether?

8. Can both measurements be right?

One large square tile covers exactly the same area as 4 small square tiles:

A mat is covered exactly by 3 large tiles, with no gaps or overlaps.

Ben measures the same mat using small tiles. How many will he need?

Can Ben and Mia correctly report different numbers of tiles for the same mat? Explain.

9. Work backwards through the machine

A number machine follows these instructions:

  1. Multiply the starting number by 3.
  2. Add 4.

The final answer is 25.

What number went into the machine? Check your answer by following both instructions forwards.

10. Find every lunch choice

Choose one sandwich and one drink.

Sandwiches: cheese, egg, chicken

Drinks: water, milk

Find every different sandwich-and-drink combination. Each option can appear in more than one combination.

To organise your search, keep one sandwich fixed, pair it with each drink, then move to the next sandwich.

How many different lunch choices are there? How do you know none are missing?
