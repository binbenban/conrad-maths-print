1. Meet one thousand

10 hundreds make 1 thousand.

You have 9 hundred-blocks and receive 3 more hundred-blocks.

What number do you have altogether? Write it:
- in digits;
- as thousands and hundreds.

2. Which is closer?

This number line shows three equally spaced numbers:

Mark approximately where 1,400 and 1,700 belong.

Which of those two numbers is closer to 1,500? Explain using the differences.

Multiplication and Division

Some ideas are new. Use drawings or objects, and explain your thinking.

3. Split a tricky fact

Three shelves each hold 8 books.

Imagine splitting each shelf’s books into 5 books and 3 books.

You know:
- 3 × 5 = 15
- 3 × 3 = 9

How can these facts help you find 3 × 8? Show how the parts fit together.

4. Packs, then presents

You have 32 stickers. Put them into packs containing 8 stickers each.

Then give away 2 full packs.

How many packs remain? Show both steps.

Area — Covering a Surface

Some ideas are new. Use drawings or objects, and explain your thinking.

Area tells us how much flat surface a shape covers. We can measure it by counting equal square tiles, with no gaps or overlaps.

5. Which mat covers more?

Each box below represents one square tile. All tiles are the same size.

Which mat has the greater area? How many more tiles does it cover?

6. Different shape, same area?

You have 12 identical square tiles.

Arrange or draw them to make two different rectangles. Use all 12 tiles each time, with no gaps or overlaps.

Do your rectangles have the same area? Explain—even though their shapes look different.

Fractions — Equal Pieces Make a Whole

Some ideas are new. Use drawings or objects, and explain your thinking.

7. Meet thirds

When a whole is divided into 3 equal parts, each part is called one third.

Draw two identical rectangular cakes. Divide each cake into thirds.

Mia eats one third of one cake. How many third-sized pieces remain altogether across both cakes?

8. Meet fifths

When a whole is divided into 5 equal parts, each part is called one fifth.

Five equal pieces make one complete paper strip. Leo has 3 of these pieces.

How many more pieces does he need to make two complete strips? Draw to explain.

Time — Will the Plan Fit?

Some ideas are new. Use drawings or objects, and explain your thinking.

9. Ready before lunch?

It is 11:20 am. Lunch starts at 12:00 noon.

You want to:
- build a model for 25 minutes;
- tidy up for 10 minutes.

Can you finish both before lunch? How many minutes will you have left before lunch starts? There is no break between the activities.

10. Choose a game

You have from 2:10 pm until 3:00 pm to play a game and pack it away.

  • Game A takes 35 minutes.
  • Game B takes 45 minutes.
  • Packing away takes 10 minutes, whichever game you choose.

Which game can you finish and pack away by 3:00 pm? Show how you checked both choices. Finishing exactly at 3:00 pm is allowed.
