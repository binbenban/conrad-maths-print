Measurement

You’ll need paper, scissors, a ruler and 12 small objects such as LEGO pieces. Explain answers aloud or show your working.

1 — Estimate, then measure

Find a spoon. Guess its length in centimetres, then measure it. How close was your guess?

2 — Read the ruler carefully

A ribbon starts at the 3 cm mark on a ruler and ends at the 11 cm mark. How long is the ribbon?

3 — Choose a sensible unit

Would you use grams or kilograms to describe the mass of:
- an apple?
- an adult?

Measurement & Equal Parts

For equal-parts questions, no fraction symbols are needed: fold, draw and explain.

4 — Fill the bottle

A cup holds 250 mL. A bottle holds 1 litre, which is 1,000 mL. How many full cups would fill the bottle?

5 — A fair share

Draw a rectangular cake. Draw one straight line to share it fairly between two people. How could you check that both pieces are the same size?

6 — Is any cut fair?

Sam says, “If I cut a sandwich into two pieces, we must get the same amount.”
Is Sam always right? Draw a picture to explain.

Maths — Explore and Review

Equal Parts & Multiplication

Use drawings or objects to show your ideas.

7 — Fold and discover

Fold a sheet of paper into two equal parts. Fold it in half again, then open it.
How many equal parts have you made? Shade one part.

8 — Which piece is bigger?

Draw two identical chocolate bars. Share one equally between 2 children and the other equally between 4 children.
Which group gives each child a bigger piece? Explain using your drawings.

9 — Rows of robots

Arrange 12 objects in equal rows, with more than one object in each row. Find two different arrangements. Write a multiplication calculation for each.

Division & Subtraction Review

Explain your strategy or show your working.

10 — Pack the cars

There are 28 toy cars. Each box holds 4 cars. How many boxes are needed? Show a multiplication fact or skip-counting that helps.

11 — Calculate and check

Work out 164 − 78. Check your answer using addition. You may use borrow, calculate, return.

12 — What was there before?

A shop sold 36 balloons and had 47 left. How many balloons did it have before the sale?
