Advanced Trigonometry and Simultaneous Equations

Show working. Use the supplied diagrams, which are not to scale. Give answers to the requested accuracy and include units. Do not round during intermediate steps.

1. Cosine rule and area

Triangle ABC has AB = 16 cm, AC = 23 cm, and ∠BAC = 58°.

(a) Find BC, to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.

(b) Find the area of triangle ABC, to the nearest square centimetre.

2. Sine rule from two angles

Triangle PQR has ∠P = 47°, ∠Q = 68°, and PQ = 22 m.

(a) Find ∠R.

(b) Find PR and QR, each to the nearest tenth of a metre.

(c) Find the area of triangle PQR, to the nearest square metre.

3. Bearings, cosine rule, and sine rule

From lighthouse L, boat A is on a bearing of 028° at a distance of 18 km. Boat B is on a bearing of 117° at a distance of 25 km.

(a) Find the distance between boats A and B.

(b) Find the bearing of B from A.

4. Three-dimensional trigonometry

A rectangular storage crate is 15 cm long, 9 cm wide, and 12 cm high.

(a) Find the length of the space diagonal, to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.

(b) Find the angle between the space diagonal and the base of the crate, to the nearest tenth of a degree.

5. Cone cross-section

A conical tent has a vertical height of 5.2 m and a circular base with diameter 8.6 m.

(a) Find the slant height of the tent, to the nearest tenth of a metre.

(b) Find the angle between the tent fabric and the ground, to the nearest degree.

(c) Find the curved surface area of the tent, excluding its base, to the nearest square metre.

6. Trapezium and diagonal

Trapezium ABCD has AB ∥ DC, AB = 31 cm, DC = 18 cm, and AD = 16 cm. Side AD makes an angle of 54° with AB. Diagonal BD is drawn.

(a) Find the perpendicular height of the trapezium.

(b) Find its area, to the nearest square centimetre.

(c) Find BD, to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.

7. Challenge — simultaneous equations with two cables

A vertical mast is held by two taut cables attached to the ground on opposite sides. One cable makes an angle of 57° with the ground and the other makes an angle of 38° with the ground. The ground anchors are 42 m apart.

Let x be the horizontal distance from the mast to the 57° anchor.

(a) Write two expressions for the mast height.

(b) Form and solve an equation to find the mast height, to the nearest tenth of a metre.

(c) Find the length of each cable.

8. Challenge — simultaneous equations with bearings

A hiker walks x km from point H on a bearing of 035°, then walks y km on a bearing of 142°. They finish 20 km due east of H.

(a) Resolve both journeys into east-west and north-south components.

(b) Form simultaneous equations for x and y.

(c) Find x and y, to the nearest tenth of a kilometre.

9. Challenge — sine rule as simultaneous equations

In triangle ABC, ∠A = 52°, ∠B = 71°, and ∠C = 57°. The lengths of sides a and b, opposite ∠A and ∠B, add to 42 cm.

(a) Use the sine rule to write an equation linking a and b.

(b) Use this equation and a + b = 42 to find a and b.

(c) Find the area of the triangle, to the nearest square centimetre.

10. Challenge — regular octagon

A regular octagonal courtyard has side length 6.4 m.

(a) By splitting the octagon into eight congruent triangles, find its apothem.

(b) Find the area of the courtyard, to the nearest square metre.

(c) A 0.80 m-wide path is built around the outside of the courtyard. Explain which measurements would need to change before calculating the area of the path.
