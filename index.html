Maths — Place Value

Think, connect and explain. Use drawings or objects when helpful.

1. Same digits, different values

Mia writes 506. Leo writes 560.

Leo says, “Our numbers use the same digits, so they must be worth the same.”

Is he right? Explain what the 6 is worth in each number, then find the difference between the two numbers.

2. A two-button puzzle

A number machine starts at 398. It has two buttons:
- Add 10
- Add 1

Make it reach 420 using as few button presses as you can. Show your presses and the numbers along the way.

You cannot subtract or reset the machine.

Maths — Mass and Capacity

Think, connect and explain. Use drawings or objects when helpful.

3. Balance the parcel

A parcel has a mass of 650 g. On the other side of a balance are weights of 200 g, 200 g and 100 g.

You have three extra weights: 50 g, 100 g and 200 g. You may use each extra weight once.

Which extra weights should you add to balance the parcel? Show how you checked.

4. Rescue the water

An empty jug holds 1 litre, which is 1,000 mL.

You have bottles containing 600 mL and 450 mL. You want to fill the jug exactly, without spilling any water.

How much water must stay in the bottles altogether? Show how you would pour the water to make this happen.

Maths — Data

Think, connect and explain. Use drawings or objects when helpful.

5. Can the leader change?

This chart shows books read by two classes.

Each circle represents 2 books.

Class A   ○ ○ ○ ○
Class B   ○ ○ ○

Class B then reads 3 more books. Class A reads no more books.

Which class has now read more books, and how many more? Explain how you used the chart’s key.

6. A chart that cannot quite fit

A pet club has 8 dogs and 6 cats.

Draw a picture chart using one circle for every 2 pets.

Then one more cat joins. Can your chart still show the exact numbers using only whole circles and the same key? Explain. Suggest one way to change the chart so it shows the new numbers exactly.

Maths — Division

Think, connect and explain. Use drawings or objects when helpful.

7. Find the missing row size

Some toy cars are arranged in 4 equal rows.

Then 6 cars are taken away, leaving 18 cars altogether.

How many cars were in each row before any were taken away? Draw or explain your steps.

8. Exactly four packs

A shop has 30 pencils. It can make packs containing 5 pencils or 10 pencils.

Can it pack all 30 pencils into exactly 4 full packs, using both pack sizes?

Draw the packs and label how many pencils are in each. No pencils may be left over.

Maths — 3D Shapes

Think, connect and explain. Use drawings or objects when helpful.

9. Explore a cylinder

Find a food tin with a flat top and bottom. Its shape is called a cylinder.

Predict what will happen when you gently push it while it is:
- standing on its flat bottom;
- lying on its curved side.

Try both on a flat surface. Which position lets it roll? Explain what is different about the surface touching the table.

10. Fix the tower plan

You have a cube-shaped block and a cylinder-shaped tin with a flat top and bottom.

Sam puts the tin on its curved side and tries to balance the block on top. The tower is wobbly.

How could you rearrange the same two objects to make a steadier tower? Find two arrangements. Explain which surfaces touch each other in each arrangement.
