Maths — A Small Step Up

Use drawings or objects when helpful. Explain your thinking aloud or show your working.

1 — Build the same amount

Draw two identical rectangular cakes. Divide one into 2 equal pieces and the other into 4 equal pieces.
Shade one piece of the first cake. How many pieces of the second cake would you shade to show the same amount?

2 — Share a collection

Use 16 counters or draw 16 dots. Share them equally among 4 children.
Two children put their shares together. How many counters do they have? Is that the same as half of all the counters?

3 — More pieces, more cake?

Two identical cakes are cut differently:
- Cake A is cut into 4 equal pieces.
- Cake B is cut into 8 equal pieces.

Mia gets one piece of Cake A. Leo gets two pieces of Cake B.
Leo says, “I have more cake because I have more pieces.” Is he right? Draw to explain.

4 — A ruler disagreement

A toy car starts at the 4 cm mark on a ruler and ends at the 13 cm mark.
A robot says it is 10 cm long because it counted both end marks. Is it correct? Draw the ruler marks to explain.

5 — Finish filling the jug

A jug holds 1 litre (1,000 mL). It already contains 400 mL of water.
How many full 200 mL cups must you add to fill it?

6 — Build, then turn

Make 3 rows of 6 counters. Write a multiplication calculation for the total.
Turn your arrangement a quarter-turn. How many rows are there now, and how many counters are in each row? Write the new multiplication calculation.

9 — Find the missing amount

A game starts with 150 points. After some points are spent, 76 points remain.
How many points were spent? Write a calculation that matches the story.

10 — Across the hour

A model-building activity starts at 10:40 am and lasts 35 minutes. What time does it finish? Show how you split the time at 11:00 am.
