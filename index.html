3. Which way costs less?

A shop sells pencils in two ways:

  • One pencil: 40c
  • One pack of 3 pencils: $1

You need exactly 6 pencils.

Compare buying six single pencils with buying two packs. Which costs less, and how much would you save?

4. Meet chance

A bag contains 4 red counters and 2 blue counters, all the same size and shape.

Without looking, you mix the counters and take out one.

  • Certain means it must happen.
  • Possible but not certain means it could happen, but might not.
  • Impossible means it cannot happen.

Use those descriptions for each statement:

  • “I will take out a blue counter.”
  • “I will take out a green counter.”
  • “I will take out either a red or a blue counter.”

5. Two moves, repeating

This pattern uses the same two moves over and over:

6, 10, 9, 13, 12, 16, ___, ___

Find the two moves and write the next two numbers.

Explain why adding the same amount every time would not fit this pattern.

6. Find the treasure

Each place occupies one square on this map:

MuseumParkPoolCafé
BakerySchoolShopBank
LibraryHomeGardenHall

The treasure is:

  • somewhere above the Library, in the same column;
  • immediately to the left of the Park.

Which place contains the treasure? Explain how both clues fit.

7. Does every corner tell the whole story?

A square corner matches the corner of a sheet of paper.

Leo says:

“If a four-sided shape has four square corners, all four sides must be the same length.”

Is he right? Draw a shape that proves your answer. You may use the corner of a sheet of paper to help.

8. Make the chances equal

A different bag contains 3 red counters and 1 blue counter, all the same size and shape.

You will mix them and take one without looking.

  • Which colour are you more likely to take?
  • You may add only blue counters. How many must you add so red and blue have an equal chance?

“Equal chance” means neither colour is more likely than the other.

9. Catch the subtraction mistake

A robot says:

143 − 58 = 95

Before calculating exactly, decide: should the answer be greater than or less than 100?

Then find the correct answer and check it with addition. You may use borrow, calculate, return.

10. Find every number

Find all the two-digit numbers that follow both rules:

  • The two digits add to 10.
  • The tens digit is greater than the ones digit.

How can you check that you have found them all?
