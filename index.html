Trigonometry: Ratios, Bearings and Models

Show a labelled sketch whenever it helps. Give answers to the requested accuracy and include units.

1. Exact values

Without a calculator, simplify:

(2 sin 30° + cos 60°) ÷ tan 45°

2. Connecting trig ratios

In a right-angled triangle, tan θ = 3/4 and 0° < θ < 90°.

Without using a calculator, find sin θ.

3. Finding sides

Triangle ABC is right-angled at B. ∠A = 38° and AB = 12 cm.

(a) Find BC, to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.

(b) Find AC, to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.

4. Bearings

Point B is 120 m east and 160 m north of point A.

(a) Find the straight-line distance from A to B.

(b) Find the true bearing of B from A.

5. Challenge — slope angle

A cycling track drops vertically by 18 m over a horizontal distance of 65 m.

(a) Find the angle the track makes with the horizontal, to the nearest degree.

(b) Find the length of the track, to the nearest metre.

6. Challenge — simultaneous equations

From point A, the angle of elevation to the top of a communications tower is 16°. After walking 75 m directly towards the tower to point B, the angle of elevation is 29°. The ground is level.

Let x be the horizontal distance from B to the tower.

(a) Write two expressions for the height of the tower in terms of x.

(b) Form and solve a simultaneous equation to find the height of the tower, to the nearest metre.

7. Challenge — simultaneous equations

A drone is hovering vertically above the top of a cliff. From point P on level ground, the angle of elevation to the drone is 24°. After walking 60 m directly towards the cliff to point Q, the angle of elevation is 41°.

Let x be the horizontal distance from Q to the point directly below the drone.

(a) Write two expressions for the drone’s height in terms of x.

(b) Use simultaneous equations to find the drone’s height, to the nearest metre.
