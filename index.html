Maths — Shapes and Symmetry

Draw or explain your thinking where helpful.

1. Shape detective

A square corner looks like the corner of a sheet of paper. Two straight edges meet without leaning towards or away from each other. You can use a paper corner to check whether a shape’s corner matches.

Draw a four-sided shape with four equal sides.
Can you draw another four-sided shape with four equal sides but no square corners?

2. Fold and match

Fold a rectangular sheet of paper so its two short edges meet exactly. Open it and trace the fold.
Does the fold split the paper into two matching parts? Find another fold that does this.

3. Make a picture chart

A picture chart uses pictures or symbols to show how many.

For example, this chart shows pets. Each ○ represents one pet:

Dogs   ○ ○ ○
Cats   ○

It shows 3 dogs and 1 cat.

Now turn the fruit table into your own picture chart. Use one circle for each vote. Which fruits are tied?

4. Could it win?

Three more children vote. Could banana finish with more votes than either other fruit? Show one way the three new votes could be shared.

5. Same digits, different numbers

Use the digits 3, 0 and 7 once each to make:
- the largest three-digit number;
- the smallest three-digit number.

Remember: a three-digit number cannot start with 0.

6. Build it differently

A number has 2 hundreds, 14 tens and 6 ones. What number is it?
Write it again using hundreds, tens and ones, with fewer than 10 tens.
